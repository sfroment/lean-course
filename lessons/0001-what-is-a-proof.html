Lesson 0001 · Foundations

What is a proof? What is Lean?

Tied to the mission: prove and discover mathematics yourself, from the base. Estimated time: 20–30 minutes.

The one idea of this lesson

A proof in Lean is a program, and Lean re-checks it for you. You cannot hand-wave: a tiny trusted core (the kernel) verifies every single proof, every time. That is what makes "I'll prove it myself someday" a real possibility instead of a hope.

1. Your mission, made concrete

You said it yourself: knowing Pythagoras' theorem is not the same as having its proof. In this workspace we rebuild mathematics from axioms in Lean — nothing is taken on faith. By the end you will not just use theorems, you will prove them, and eventually discover new ones.

2. What Lean is

Lean 4 is two things at once:

  • a programming language (you already know several — this one just also understands logic), and
  • a proof assistant: you write down a claim and a proof, and Lean checks the proof mechanically.

The crucial design fact: the kernel — a small, simple, trusted piece of code — re-verifies every proof from scratch. Tactics and automation are just helpers that build proof terms; if they lie or bug out, the kernel catches it. There is an escape hatch called sorries… spelled sorry, and it always leaves a visible warning. You can't cheat silently.

3. Terms, types, propositions

Three ideas carry all of Lean:

  1. Every term (every expression) has a type. 2 + 2 has type Nat (natural numbers).
  2. A proposition like 2 + 2 = 4 is itself a term — its type is Prop, "the type of propositions".
  3. A proof of a proposition P is just a term whose type is P. That's the whole trick. Prop is not special magic; it's a type like any other.

4. Hands-on: your first Lean file

Your playground lives in playground/ (a tiny Lean project, no libraries yet — we add mathlib only when we start real math). Open playground/Playground/Basic.lean and read it, then run:

cd playground
lake env lean Playground/Basic.lean

It contains:

#check 2 + 2        -- 2 + 2 : Nat
#eval 2 + 2         -- 4

example : 2 + 2 = 4 := rfl

example : True := trivial
  • #check e asks "what is the type of e?" — here Nat.
  • #eval e computes e — here 4.
  • example : P := proof states proposition P and gives its proof. 2 + 2 = 4 holds by rfl (reflexivity of equality): both sides compute to the same thing.
  • True is the trivially-true proposition; trivial proves it.

If the file runs with no errors, you have written two machine-checked proofs. Try breaking one on purpose: change rfl to trivial on the first example and re-run — watch Lean reject it. That rejection is the proof checker working.
